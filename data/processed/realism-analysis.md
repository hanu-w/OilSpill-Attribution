# Realism Analysis — Real AIS-derived activity vs OceanWatch synthetic fleet

**Phase 4.8 deliverable.** Compares the real 8-day AIS snapshot
(2026-08-20 → 08-27, Gulf of Kutch + adjacent Arabian Sea) against the
synthetic fleet generated by `Frontend/src/simulation/` (vesselGenerator,
maritimeNetwork, trailGenerator). Goal: calibrate the demo, not replace it.

Raw numbers come from `data/processed/quality-report.json` and the processed
dataset; synthetic constants come from `maritimeNetwork.ts` /
`vesselGenerator.ts` (30 base vessels, `SIMULATION_SEED=20260827`).

---

## 2. Fishing grounds — good positional agreement

Real fishing-event positions vs synthetic `FISHING_GROUNDS` centers:

- 39 real fishing events, **11 distinct fishing vessels**, median **51 km**
  from the nearest synthetic ground center; **32/39 (82 %) within 100 km**.
- Events assigned to synthetic grounds: gulfMouth 14, offMandvi 11, offOkha 5,
  southOffshore 4, offPorbandar 3, offDiu 2.
- Dominant real hotspot is the **Gulf-of-Kutch mouth / northern gulf**
  (~22.5–22.75°N, 69.5–70.1°E) — exactly the zone `gulfMouth` + `offMandvi`
  cover.
- Outliers are physical: MATSYA VRUSHTI off Mumbai (~19°N, 72–73°E), RAZVI VIII
  north of Kachchh (~23.3°N, 68.5°E) — plausible grounds outside the demo area.
- **Vessel identities are real and local** (SHANTI SARVEKSHAK-1, M V BALAJI,
  SAI KRUBA, M V SHREE SAGAR, BETHLAHEM, BIBLE SABU, MT ZARQA, MT MANALI III).
  Note: several "MT" prefixed names are typed `fishing` by GFW while at/near
  Kandla — possible model mislabel of tanker lay-ups as fishing; treat the
  fishing-type field with mild suspicion, the positions as genuine.

**Synthetic verdict:** fishing behavior is realistic in *where* fishing
happens; the synthetic grounds could be nudged a few km northeast into the
gulf mouth to match the observed cluster.

## 3. Loitering / patrol — geographic divergence (coverage effect)

- 767 real loitering events, 127 vessels. Top 0.5° hotspot cells are all
  **off Maharashtra** (18.6–19.6°N, 71.0–72.3°E): offshore Mumbai and the
  Bombay High oilfield. Top loiterers are Coast Guard / offshore-support
  names (P.T.VENUGOPAL, COLONEL S.P.WAHI, L J JOHNSON, SHAURYA, K D MALAVIYA;
  TRITON HARMONY, CANARA PRIDE, PRIYA TWENTY FIVE).
- The Gulf of Kutch itself shows **little GFW loitering** in this window.
- Synthetic `PATROL_CENTERS` (Kandla / Mundra / Porbandar circular circuits)
  are a reasonable modeling choice for the demo, but GFW's loitering signal
  does **not** independently evidence gulf patrol circuits — the real
  loitering economy is the offshore-industry belt off Mumbai/Bombay High,
  which is outside the demo. Do not over-interpret gulf patrol loitering as
  the dominant real pattern.

## 4. Deep-lane / foreign traffic — real and present

- 82 in-region foreign-flag events (71 loitering, 11 port-visits): PAN 18,
  LBR 21, MHL 27, MLT 9, SGP 6, ARE 1.
- Genuine international names: tankers/chemical (STOLT BELUGA, FLAMINIA,
  CHEM MELBOURNE, ATHANASIA C, AQUARUBY, AUREUS), container (CMA CGM LA
  SCALA, MSC BRIANNA, WINPOSH REGENT), bulk (DENSA DOLPHIN, PISCES LEADER),
  offshore support / drillships (ADMARINE 9/10/11, SEAMEC AGASTYA/PALADIN,
  DIVINE DRILLER, ARMADA STERLING, SKANDI GIANT, TRIDENT II, SEA CHALLENGER,
  VIRTUE-1), Jindal group (JINDAL SUPREME/STAR/EXPLORER), and others
  (MOTELE OLA, DEEPSEA FORTUNE, YASA GOLDENBOSPHORUS).
- **Reading:** the synthetic Karachi ↔ Mumbai `deep` lane is realistic — real
  foreign tonnage does transit/loiter the corridor. The foreign *sample* is
  capped (≤ 2,500/query) so it under-counts foreign traffic; and it captured
  more Mumbai offshore support vessels than Vadinar VLCC calls (only 1 VADINAR
  visit, 0 foreign at Vadinar in the sample). Identity coverage of foreign
  traffic is good; exhaustive enumeration is not claimed.

## 5. Anchorage / laid-up vessels — the model under-represents volume

- 570 long-duration events (≥ 168 h = entire window) — laid-up / parked
  tonnage holding anchorages (e.g. 11-year "visits" from GFW merged port
  visits). Median per-vessel event span is 178 h of a ~191 h window; median
  window activity 157 h — most detected vessels are present most of the time.
- Synthetic model has **3** stationary `anchored` vessels (Kandla / Porbandar /
  Mumbai roadsteads). Real anchorages hold far more stationary tonnage
  (969 vessels total; 588 single-event, 253 with 4+ events). For a realism
  bump, the synthetic fleet could add a handful of low-speed roadstead
  drifters — but this does not affect the INC-2026-001 scenario.

## 6. Data quality — distinguishing signal from artifact

Only genuine-position checks were counted as errors; artifacts are explained,
not silently dropped:

| Check | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Invalid coordinates | 0 | clean |
| Duplicate event ids | 1 / 16,662 | dropped, negligible |
| On-land events | 481 (477 = port visits) | **Expected**: anchorage on tidal flats mapped as land by the coarse grid; positions match real port coords (see §1) |
| Not safe water | 1,248 | Nearshore anchorages in shallow zones; same cause |
| Segment land crossings | 607 | Sparse-event coastal navigation (sailing around coasts) — expected |
| Impossible speed jumps | 7 | **Event-modeling artifacts**: anchorage *start* position paired with *departure* time of the prior visit → false high implied speed (max 73 kn). 6 vessels. Not real teleports |
| Overlapping-event artifacts | 142 | Two event windows sharing a timestamp boundary — detection overlap, not bad positions |
| Large geographic jumps | 4 | Same port-visit boundary cause |
| Max implied speed | 73.3 kn | Bounded; physically impossible only under the artifact interpretation above |

**Bottom line:** the underlying AIS positions are trustworthy (they match real
anchorages to within km). Every flagged anomaly traces to GFW's *event*
geometry — coarse single positions per long activity — not to corrupt
observations. A real continuous-track AIS feed would not exhibit these
artifacts; they exist because this snapshot is events, not tracks.

## 7. Scenario suitability (INC-2026-001)

- The demo region (Gulf of Kutch: Kandla / Mundra / Sikka / Vadinar) is
  confirmed real, active, and positioned correctly by real data.
- The synthetic deep lane and gulf approach routes match real traffic lanes.
- Real evidence for a spill-correlation exercise (a suspect vessel that passed
  near the spill at spill time) is **present** — 381 multi-event vessels with
  ≤ 48 h gaps — but as *coarse event points*, not the dense trails the
  attribution UI animates. The snapshot is ideal for **calibrating identities,
  port coordinates, dwell times, and traffic lanes**; it is not a substitute
  for the synthetic underway tracks that drive the demo's trail playback.
